# Output design

How a converted document should look. Research on 2026-10-14 (four parallel passes: restaurant menus, retail weekly ads, price lists and fee schedules, brand theming); sources at the end. Andrei's verdict on the first raw output: "looks chaotic, nobody wants it". The rule since then: correct is not enough, the page must look like something the business would link instead of its PDF.

## Principles
- **Templates chosen, never generated.** A small AI "brief" per document picks the template and its parameters; the renderer stays deterministic. Every renderer change is verified again: saved runs rescored, axe and a keyboard pass on sample documents, conditions read with their prices, and a real screen-reader pass. What fills a template (conditions mapped to prices, crops, reading order, hidden text) is checked on every document (see Checks per document); no automated check settles accessibility on its own.
- **Show only what the PDF shows.** Words in the text layer that draw nothing (under a shape, a sticker or a cover) are not published. no invented was-prices, percentages, notices or "Free"; the report can point out what is missing (no allergen notice, no unit price), the page never adds it.
- **Brand fidelity first (Andrei, 2026-10-15):** the page is the business's brand, so its fonts, colours, hierarchy and drawn rules follow the PDF as truthfully as possible, measured from the PDF itself, never guessed; accessibility corrects (4.5:1 for every text, 1rem minimum, reflow) and never replaces the identity.
- **One self-contained file:** the PDF's own families are embedded when they are open-licensed (OFL or Apache, vendored unmodified from Google Fonts with their licence, `pnpm fonts:add`), at most 4 files and 150 KB per document; a commercial family gets a recognised free stand-in or its class stack. The printed view (below) may also use a font embedded in the PDF itself, only where that font's own embedding flag permits it (Andrei, 2026-10-17; the flag governs embedding in documents, not a web licence: a commercial face can still need a separate webfont licence, a risk accepted with the decision); the text view never does.
- **The scorer's DOM stays:** `section[data-page]` + `h2`, `.products > li`, `.price` / `.qualifier`, h3–h6, tables, `img[alt]`, `[data-review]`; no CSS `content:` text, no CSS `order` (reading order must match visual order).

## The printed view (Andrei, 2026-10-17)
- On a screen wide enough for the body text to read at 7 px (never below 800 px; Andrei, 2026-10-18: large sheets such as Bud & Alley's, Lexington and La Segunda look like their print, not reflowed), the page shows the PDF 1:1, fitted to the window (pinch to read closer; fitting measures at the 12 px width, `check:page` and contrast's large-text level at the narrowest; known limit: a browser's minimum font size, a low-vision setting, enlarges the smallest lines off their printed spots): the printed page rendered without the words we place back, our real text placed where it is printed (each printed line on its own: its face, size, ink, alignment), pictures over their printed place (transparent, alt kept). A switch "As printed / Text only" sits first in the page; forced colours, print, zoom past the threshold and "Text only" show the reflowed text view. Both views are one document: the same words in the same order (checked on every build).
- A page whose placed text overflows its printed box or collides with other placed text (measured at build time) shows the text view: text in the wrong place misinforms. A page whose backdrop does not render in time shows the text view too.
- Contrast: the printed ink is drawn as printed: its colour at its printed opacity over the ground under it (Gigi's white at 84 % reads cream), and its printed outline or shadow, taken out of the backdrop, drawn round our letters (La Segunda's red outline, Kenny & Ziggy's black copies). It is kept where it reads (4.5:1, 3:1 for text that is large at the narrowest printed width), the outline or shadow counting as the letters' edge. Else, on a flat backdrop, the ink stays as printed (2026-10-18, Andrei: Howies' gold prices darkened to brown read as the wrong colour; the text view, one switch away and the phone's view, is the conforming version); else a halo in the ground's own colour (the cells' mean), dense at the letters; never a colour moved to a contrast target, which turns dark round a gold or grey word and smears it (2026-10-19, Howies' "Vegan"). Flat (2026-10-18): the ground is read from the 8 pt cells under the letters' bodies (the word's box inset by a quarter of its height top and bottom; a cell counts when half of it lies within), their means within 0.08 of each other and their extremes no more than 0.35 apart, so a hairline or a panel's edge through a cell is no texture, and a QR code's row or the page below the baseline is not the word's ground (Howies' gold prices by their frames keep their gold instead of taking a halo; BOOK NOW reads as printed on its navy band; halos on the 33 rebuilt documents counted in the commit).
- Chrome (2026-10-18): in the printed view the view switch is one small muted line, right-aligned (an owner sees the menu, not controls), and each page's "Sections ↑" link shows only on keyboard focus; the text view keeps both as they were.
- Inline inks (2026-10-18): a word printed in another brand ink inside a placed line (Almond's red farm names, Claudio's red "&") shows that ink where it reads under its own box by the test its line passes as printed, or where the ground under it is flat (2026-10-19: Howies' gold gin names turned black inside their lines while whole gold lines stayed gold); else it keeps its line's colour, never moved or haloed on its own. The line's ink is that of its unmarked words (Almond's black "Ramen" after the red "Micky's"). Each word's colour is the one drawn, named by the brand ink it matches: the text layer can put a colour on the wrong letters (Kenny & Ziggy's orange on C and P, its gold "&" dark). A word's ink is read at its first letter, so one whose first glyph alone is printed in another ink would take that ink (no case in the 33 documents). Not yet: a word in an ink that is no brand ink (black codes or marks such as +, *, ♥ inside a coloured line): 30 of 5,032 placed lines.
- Faces per word (2026-10-20, Andrei on Howies' drinks: "GAELIC" light, every whisky "(40%)" bold and black): a placed line takes the PDF's own program most of its letters are printed in, and a word printed in another weight, style or program of the same family keeps its own (a `.face` span carrying its program first, its stand-in after, its stem-matched weight and its style; printed view only, inert in the text view). Another family's word keeps the line's face, and a word keeps its line's size (a small-caps line prints its tails smaller). A line whose own programs cannot draw one of its parts takes its stand-ins whole. Small caps a role asks for are synthesised on a stand-in (`font-synthesis: small-caps`; weight and style are never synthesised), the PDF's own face prints its own.
- Faces (2026-10-18): each placed line takes the face most of its letters are printed in: the family when vendored, its lookalike, else a vendored stand-in of its typeface class (geometric Jost, oldstyle EB Garamond, transitional Caladea, didone Bodoni Moda, slab Roboto Slab, humanist Lato, neo-grotesque Arimo, condensed Roboto Condensed); Times keeps the desktop's Times; brush scripts take Yellowtail and hand-lettering Kalam (printed view only: in the text view a display face may reach dish names). A family neither named nor classed keeps its role's face. The PDF's own face comes first where its maker permits embedding (2026-10-18; every embedded program of that PostScript name installable or editable, read from the raw program since pdf.js rewrites the flag): the program pdf.js rebuilt, its character map written from the PDF's ToUnicode (one letter per code, the glyph pdf.js draws), declared for the letters it has; a line keeps it only when it draws every letter the line shows (checked at build on the rendered text), else the whole line takes the stand-in. Bannatyne's Crimsons and FeelingPassionate print as printed. A face draws only its file's weights (no synthesis). The files sit beside the page (`fonts/`) under their own names, fetched only by the printed view. A lookalike or stand-in is sized and weighted to the printed face (2026-10-18, Andrei on Howies: "font family, size and colours differ"): at build each printed face pdf.js can rebuild is measured in a browser beside its lookalike on the letters its subset has (`eval/facemetrics.ts`, into backdrop.json `metrics`): the size scale blends the x-height ratio (flat lowercase letters) and the cap-height ratio by the line's share of lowercase letters, so running text takes the printed x-height and a price or a capitals heading the printed cap height (Brandon → Montserrat 0.76 for lowercase, 1.0 for capitals; a geometric mean left Howies' white running text 17 % too tall); the weight is the lookalike's whose stem (the longest ink run across the middle of the x-height band on l, i, I, H…) is nearest the printed one (Brandon Bold → Montserrat 750, Kabel Medium → Jost 400), never under 300 on text below 24 px; a scale outside 0.7–1.25 is a mis-measure and leaves the line as before. Tracking stays in printed points, and a one-line text left narrower than its box by over 5 % is spread to it with letter spacing up to 0.25 em (fit.ts `--lx`); the box is the piece's own words' (rows below), so a tab or another leaf's words no longer stretch it ("France, 14%" read "F r a n c e" until 2026-10-20). A heading (1.4 × the page's body size) in a family with no vendored twin or lookalike, only a class stand-in or a hand-lettering face, stays printed in the backdrop, ours read (Kenny & Ziggy's BinnerD, Deli Script and Oz Handicraft titles; the owner sees the heading first). A printed face whose flat lowercase letters stand as tall as its capitals draws capitals for lowercase (Howies' Lulo Clean): its words show in capitals in both views, and its own file answers to both cases of each letter, so a line is never dropped for a case the PDF's codes did not use.
- Rows (2026-10-20, Andrei on Howies' wine lists: "not all of them start from the left as they should, and this makes the html asymmetrical"): a placed text is a piece of words printed side by side; a text whose cited words leave a gap (another leaf's words between them, or an icon cited before the name it follows) is cut there, each piece at its own words. Pieces of one printed row within a gap of 1 em are laid out as ours draws them with the printed gaps between them, the row set on the edge (left, centre, right) its printed extent shares within 0.05 em with the most texts it overlaps in its column; a tie or none keeps it centred. A row's fixed points are the drawings the backdrop keeps (a piece meets its first drawing, a piece ending on a drawing ends where it is printed) and its prices (they stay where placed); the other pieces flow from the nearest. Our letters move inside the printed box (`--ti`), never the box, so the collision check keeps its meaning, and never leave it: a word's place is printed (the gate weighs it there), so a narrower stand-in's slack stays inside its own box, and one wider than its box stays where the browser draws it. A gap wider than 1 em between two words of one text cuts it, a heavily tracked heading too: each of its words then stands at its own printed place (measured, no regression).
- Baseline: each placed line stands on its printed baseline (its first word's first letter, the ink over any printed copy, measured from the PDF), set at build in the browser, whatever its face's ascent or its box's height; a printed baseline more than half a line from ours is not trusted (2026-10-18).
- Letter spacing: each word's printed tracking is measured from its font's advance widths in the PDF (pdf.js) and set in ems on its line (BANNATYNE 0.18 em, La Segunda's RICH IN TRADITION 0.13 em); text below 24 px is never set tighter than its face. The placed box grows by one spacing, which follows the last letter too.
- The backdrop keeps the boxes and strips behind the words (their own ground) and loses the glyphs we place back and the copies that draw them again (outlines, shadows, extrusions: fitted to the printed letters, not ours; we draw them on ours). Words drawn inside a picture under an invisible text layer stay as printed. So do words printed at an angle (a rubber stamp, a slanted note) and words in a hand we cannot draw (a signature whose font may not be embedded and has no stand-in): ours is read, not shown (2026-10-18). A copy of the words is a shape under or round the words it mostly covers, not one a neighbour's zone merely touches (Kenny & Ziggy's red badges round its digits stay), and never a shape an opaque box drawn later hides (its wood grain under the yellow cards: not a shadow of the card's text). A glyph on an unplaced word's baseline stays even inside the placed line below's zone (tight lines; a validator-flagged line stays printed whole).
- WCAG 1.4.12 (text spacing): placed lines keep their printed box, so user spacing overrides can clip them; "Text only" is the conforming alternative.

## The brief (one model call per document)
Input: a contact sheet of the first pages and the cover. Output, schema-validated:
- `type`: `menu | retail | pricelist | brochure | mixed` (+ per-page type for mixed documents);
- `title`: the business and document name as printed, logo text included ("The Carew Inn · Evening Menu");
- `brand`: which sampled colours are the brand's (background, ink, accent) versus photos; hex values are snapped to the colours sampled at the text and contrast-checked, never taken as given;
- `headings`: the heading type class (old style, transitional, didone, slab, geometric, neo-grotesque, humanist, condensed, script);
- `logo`: page and box, for the title band;
- `validity`: dates printed for offers ("Valid 8–14 Oct");
- `tone`: formal / casual / premium → density and spacing.
The user confirms type and title at upload (one click to correct), as for layout. Accuracy is measured on the corpus (page types and layout labels already exist) before it is trusted. Cost: one small vision call per document, negligible next to page conversion.

## Templates

### Menu
- One h1 (business + menu name), h2 per section in the PDF (rendered h3 under the page marker), dishes as `ul > li`.
- Row: name left, price right on the same baseline, description below in a quieter colour; no dot leaders; column width capped (~40rem) so the price stays near the name; two columns (CSS columns, `break-inside: avoid`) on wide screens, one on phones. A list the PDF prints one dish per line (items stacked, the median item at least half the page wide) stays one column everywhere; when most descriptions run on from the name, the name, description and price share the line (the lead description before the price; with inline prices, after them), otherwise rows are capped at 44rem (2026-10-16, `html/shapes.ts`).
- Prices one way per list: if any amount in the list has pence or cents, all show two decimals; otherwise none ("£1,040" stays whole, "£7,500.00" shows "£7,500"). Tabular figures; not coloured.
- Several prices are labelled ("Small 9 · Large 14"); the label sits where it is printed ("Bowl 8", "3.5 ea"), as a muted word in the body size, never a badge; a separator the print puts after the pair ("ea," "20,") stands between the prices, outside them. Wine by glass/bottle as a real table; a two-column table without headers whose second column is only prices (Carmine's beer lists) renders as menu rows (2026-10-16, `html/table.ts`).
- Add-ons ("Add 3 king prawns £5") as a smaller indented line under their dish, their amounts set apart (600 weight, full text colour). Known gap: the amounts are not price elements yet, because the scorer reads every price element in a dish as the dish's price; they become price elements once keys and scorer carry an add-on role.
- Dietary marks: on a menu whose legend is one printed block of "code words" lines, the codes ending a description move to one slot after the dish name as outlined tags (14 px, neutral, a border so they survive forced colours), each read out with the legend's own words (", Gluten Free Option", visually hidden). The legend is repeated once before the first section; the printed one stays. Codes no legend defines stay as printed. US raw/undercooked asterisks kept with their warning.
- Dietary icons (2026-10-18, `pdf/icons.ts`): a menu that marks dishes with small drawings instead of codes (Howies' wheat, drop, nut, V and leaf) names them from its own legend, a line of icon-and-label pairs read from the vector layer (the same drawings at the same size, within 1 pt). Each named icon becomes the label's words in the text layer at its printed spot, in place ("( gluten free available)", "dairy free gluten free Roast baby courgette salad"): moved after the name a condition would change its meaning. The text view shows them as the code tags; the printed view keeps the drawing in the backdrop and gives the tag the drawing's width with its words read, not shown; a line holding one reads as one text (role="text"). Icons no legend names stay drawings (Bernini's 12 unkeyed rasters): to assess.
- A note printed right under a menu-style list stays where the PDF has it, in 600 weight. When its first naming word names some items ("All burgers served with…"), it describes those items (`aria-describedby` on each) and nothing is grouped; when it names none ("Prices per person, based on two people sharing"), it describes the whole list and shares one outlined group with it (1 px neutral rule, 12–16 px padding). When it starts with the heading it sits under ("TEA TIME SET MENU: two courses £31.90…"), it prices every list since that heading and stands without a box.
- Photos: none per dish; at most one atmosphere image (the cover photo) in the band. Brochure-like photo pages render as a gallery.
- Sticky section links (Starters · Mains · Sides) on long menus, `scroll-margin-top` so headings are not hidden.
- Type: scale 1.25, body 17–18px, two weights (400/600), heading stack from the brief.

### Retail (weekly ads, leaflets, catalogues)
- Card, in code order: image → name → offer (current price, was price, amount off, unit price) → details and badges. A screen reader hears the name, then the price.
- Cards in one row share their rows (CSS subgrid: picture, name, offer, details), so prices line up across a row without moving the details before the price.
- One dominant price (1.75–2.25 × the name, bold, tabular); was price as visible text ("was £3.00", "statt 9,90 €"), never strikethrough alone; unit price on every card where printed, small but AA (UK Price Marking Order, German PAngV).
- Deals are real text badges in one accent colour ("2 for £5", "Lidl Plus price", an amount off, a short all-caps printed badge such as "AKTION" on a card with a was price); loyalty prices show both prices. Never compute a reduction.
- Grid: cards at least 14rem wide (at most four in the 64rem column), decided by the list's own width (container query), not the viewport; below two columns (29rem) each card is a row: a 6rem picture beside name and price, details across the full width under them.
- Images: square box, `object-fit: contain`, light background. Acceptance: the product whole, no badge cut, no word and no visible part of another offer. The product's own words reaching out of its picture (its price, name or a badge printed on the photo) are taken in whole along their line, with a few pixels to spare, also when taking one in reaches another; another offer's words and pictures at the edge are cut away only within a 25 % edge band and never through the product's own words. A box that stops inside its picture (a badge's top) grows up or down while the next rows still show picture content, crossing small gaps, never into another offer, a full-width photo or band. Words are measured by their painted extent in the text colour the PDF gives them, not by their font box.
- Navigation: h2 per leaflet section, section chips that wrap and never scroll (a label wider than the screen wraps inside its chip; pinned to the top only on screens of 60rem and more while the labels total 90 characters or fewer; more than 8 sections fold behind "Sections (n)" on a phone; a "Sections ↑" link closes every page), validity banner once per page set.

### Price list (fees, tariffs, rates, service lists)
- Semantic tables: caption, `th scope="col"`, the item as `th scope="row"`; unit or VAT basis in the header.
- Money columns right-aligned (header too), tabular figures, same decimals in a column; text columns left; horizontal rules only, a heavier rule under the header, no vertical borders, no zebra.
- Header, content, tail (2026-10-16): on the first page, the leading blocks before the first section that lie in the top fifth of the page and are a logo, a heading that names the place or is at least twice the body, or a line of up to twelve words (tagline, "EST. 1948", address) form the header, printed as a masthead before the section navigation (the name first, as on paper): always centred and set apart with 2.5rem above and below (Andrei, 2026-10-16: the header is separate from the columns), the logo at its printed share of the page width (8rem to 60%), the name at its printed ratio to the body text, bounded to 1.5–4.5rem and 11vw (a newspaper title still fits a phone) and kept outside the section headings' compression; the band then names the document for screen readers only. Trailing paragraphs below 85% of a page or set smaller than the body (advisories, notices) are the page's tail, rendered as small print after the sections. Only with a measured brand; a page without one renders as before.
- Sections in columns (2026-10-16, `html/layout.ts`): a section is a block-level heading with what follows it; its column is where its heading is printed, and whether its items flow across the page decides its shape. Consecutive narrow sections cluster by heading position into a grid of up to four columns on screens of 60rem and more (stacked in print order below), with no multi-column list inside a column; a section whose items flow across more than 60% of the page is a full-width row with the usual two-column list; such a row starting to the right of a single narrow column stands beside it (a side band, one third and two thirds). Three or more printed columns widen the page to 80rem. A dish's price printed right after its name (Moosewood) stays inline; otherwise it sits at the column's edge. Only with a measured brand.
- Prices as printed (US menus, 2026-10-16): a page that prints no currency anywhere shows its bare numbers as printed ("19"); market price ("MP", "MKT"), two-size prices ("22/32"), rising sizes with a sign or cents ("$65/80/90", "3.99/4.99/5.99"; in review with no size label) and a price per unit with a sign or cents ("$30/dz", "$48/2 dz") stay as printed, a date ("10/16/2026") or a measure ("1/2 lb") never; digits a letter-spaced text layer splits ("21.9" + "5") are joined back ("21.95"). Only a leaflet that prints fused euro/cent pairs needs a currency and keeps bare digits in review. A price cited twice for one dish is shown once. A dish with several priced sizes ("Flute 9 · Bellini 10 · Btl 25") wraps its prices on a phone within 60% of the row (`fit-content`), so the dish name is never squeezed to a letter a line; each price keeps its label on one line.
- Printed case wins: when the model's text differs from the cited words only by case ("Raw Bar" for "RAW BAR"), the page shows the cited words; the model's text stands in only for a broken text layer ("Truffle" for "Truf e"). A ligature the font maps to no text splits a word on one line with a 0.35–1 em gap (a letter or comma before, a small letter after): the model's fi, fl, ff, ffi, ffl, ft, ti or tt there is accepted and listed as letters read from the image (Gigi "Pla er", Noah's "Truf e"); any other difference is an error.
- Words split by a dropped ligature (2026-10-18): where the PDF gives the drawn glyph its replacement text (/ActualText, Apple Pages: Gigi, Noah's) and Poppler kept a copy of it at the page's edge, the letters join their word before the model reads the page ("Pancetta", "Biscotti", "3.5oz filets"), checked against the PDF like any printed word; the copy is hidden. A dropped glyph glued to a word with no replacement text is a validator error unless the model read the word: the leaf is flagged, the printed view shows the print, the text view our words.
- Letters drawn as shapes (2026-10-18): a letter its font subset lacks, drawn as rectangles where spaces stand (Kenny & Ziggy's "[H]ungarian", "OFFICE [H]ITS", "T[H]E BENNYS"), and digits drawn after a printed one (La Segunda's "3" + a drawn ".99") leave the word or price unwhole. A shape in the word's own ink, 0.4–1.3 of its box tall, on its baseline and within 0.12 of its height of its left or right edge marks the word as lost (`pdf/drawnletters.ts`, measured 0.03–0.08 on those, 0.20–0.24 for K&Z's nav stars): a text or price citing it is an error, flagged, so the printed view shows the print. The text view keeps the words as read until the letter is recovered (ROADMAP).
- Prices as printed (US menus, 2026-10-16): a page that prints no currency anywhere shows its bare numbers as printed ("19"), market price ("MP", "MKT"), two-size prices ("22/32"), rising sizes with a sign or cents ("$65/80/90", "3.99/4.99/5.99"; in review with no size label) and a price per unit with a sign or cents ("$30/dz", "$48/2 dz") stay as printed, a date ("10/16/2026") or a measure ("1/2 lb") never, and digits a letter-spaced text layer splits ("21.9" + "5") are joined back ("21.95"). Only a leaflet that prints fused euro/cent pairs needs a currency and keeps bare digits in review. A price cited twice for one dish is shown once.
- Printed case wins: when the model's text differs from the cited words only by case ("Raw Bar" for "RAW BAR"), the page shows the cited words; the model's text stands in only for a broken text layer ("Truffle" for "Truf e"). A ligature the font maps to no text splits a word on one line with a 0.35–1 em gap (a letter or comma before, a small letter after): the model's fi, fl, ff, ffi, ffl, ft, ti or tt there is accepted and listed as letters read from the image (Gigi "Pla er", Noah's "Truf e"); any other difference is an error.
- Inline brand colours (2026-10-16, `html/profile.ts`, `html/source.ts`): words printed in a second colour inside a role (Almond's red farm names, Carmine's crimson notes) keep it, made readable by lightness alone and never swapped for another colour; a colour counts when it lies 0.15 (OKLab) from the role's own and one of the two has chroma ≥ 0.06; colours follow the shown text letter by letter. A dark colour with chroma under 0.06 prints black and takes the page's text colour on a light page. Only with a measured brand.
- Phones: cells never break inside a word ("2026" stays whole), row headers at least 9rem and sticky at the left while the table scrolls inside its focusable region (allowed by WCAG reflow), never cards. Leading header rows are a real `thead`. A table without a caption is named by the heading it sits under, so two tables never share a name.
- Long documents: a visible contents list at the top (up to six entries visible on any document of three pages or more; longer ones behind a summary except on price lists), "Contents ↑" after each section, a sticky side contents on ≥1024px; notes numbered and placed under their table.
- Page markers carry the PDF's page number ("Page 4" for the PDF's page 4, also in the id); effective dates and a link to the original PDF near the top.
- Type: tables 16px; editorial pages of a price list read like a brochure: paragraphs at most 65ch, 17px/1.6, h3 26px/1.2, h4 20px, 24px before and 12px after headings.

### Brochure / editorial
- Readable text column (60–75ch), headings from the brand class, pull-quotes and cover as the band.
- Pictures keep their printed weight: a lone picture wider than 60 % of the page spans the column (max 40rem), a lone one under a third of the page width or a decorative one stays small (7.5rem) on its own line, pictures printed together are tiles (two or more per row, 16 px apart); no grey frame, no forced ratio. A picture printed in the same place on other pages (a running drawing, a logo) is page furniture: small, on its own line, `alt=""`. A picture merely repeated elsewhere (a product photo, a diagram) keeps its alt.

## Theme
- Measured per role from the words each element cites (pdftohtml gives every text line its font, size and colour): dish names, prices, descriptions, text and table headers take their own face, colour and size relative to the body text; headings take the style they are printed in, grouped by signature (face, size, colour), whatever level the model gave them, so a red stencil section title and a black condensed heading stay distinct. The accent is the most-used non-neutral heading colour.
- The page follows the PDF's background: white when no colour holds most of the page (a leaflet's panels are not its page), the PDF's own dark colour when it is a dark document (Carew). No automatic dark scheme: one `:root`, `color-scheme` set to the document's polarity; browser dark modes, Smart Invert and forced colours still apply. A theme switch belongs in Customize page (ROADMAP).
- Every text/background pair gated: WCAG 2 ≥ 4.5:1 for all text, text in controls included; 3:1 only for large text and non-text parts (borders, focus ring, icons); APCA Lc logged (≥ 60 body); on a brand fill, black or white text by contrast, never assumed. Every text role, headings included, at 4.5:1 (clamp() shrinks large text on phones); a colour that fails is moved in OKLab lightness, keeping its hue.
- `forced-colors: active` respected; no meaning by colour alone.
- Heading stacks per class (modernfontstacks.com): old style `'Iowan Old Style', 'Palatino Linotype', 'URW Palladio L', P052, serif`; transitional `Charter, 'Bitstream Charter', 'Sitka Text', Cambria, serif`; slab `Rockwell, 'Rockwell Nova', 'Roboto Slab', 'DejaVu Serif', serif`; geometric `Avenir, Montserrat, Corbel, 'URW Gothic', sans-serif`; humanist `Seravek, 'Gill Sans Nova', Ubuntu, Calibri, sans-serif`; neo-grotesque `Inter, Roboto, 'Helvetica Neue', Arial, sans-serif`; script → headings only, else a matching serif. Used when the PDF's family is not vendored; body `system-ui` then. A display or stencil face only for headings rendered at 1.5rem or more and at most 6 words; `font-synthesis: none`.
- Alignment as printed (2026-10-16, `html/align.ts`): a list whose items' first lines share a centre while their left edges wander (more than 6 pt) is centred, one sharing a right edge is right-set, each kept in one column with name, description and price on one line; a heading printed centred over its list is centred. Measured on 24 documents: 27 centred lists (Rucola, Osteria, Ridgway, Frankie's), 3 right-set (Noah's), the rest left. A band left visible joins the centred header. Only with a measured brand.
- Frames and panels (2026-10-16, `pdf/vectors.ts`, `html/boxes.ts`): the boxes a PDF draws round its sections are read from its vector layer, not guessed from the render: stroked rectangles and filled rings are frames (colour and rule as printed, double rules kept, decorative so never recoloured), fills are panels. Each block belongs to the smallest box holding it; a heading set in the gap of a frame's top edge stays in the border (centred when printed centred). A frame holding most of the page is the page's frame, drawn round the page section. A panel where the page's text does not read shows its words in their printed colour at 4.5:1, the fill darkening a little when that colour is already at its end (Sarge's white on red); one that still fails stays a frame. Boxes wrap inside a column or round a whole band, never across columns. Padding .75rem, 1.25rem between boxes, the PDF's density kept on a phone. Only with a measured brand.
- Logo as printed (2026-10-17, `pdf/art.ts`): the brief says on which page and where the logo or name artwork is printed; the vector layer gives its exact box (vector drawings or a raster, whichever the page draws). When the model gave no figure for it, it leads the masthead, centred, at its printed share of the width, decorative (the name is the band's h1); words printed inside it stay part of the picture. Figures snap to the picture they show instead of the model's approximate box. Only with a measured brand.
- Rules as printed (2026-10-16, `pdf/vectors.ts`, `html/lines.ts`): straight strokes and thin bars from the vector layer, in their colour, thickness (1–6 px) and style (solid, dotted, dashed, double). A rule within a line under a heading is that heading's own (centred and at its printed share of the column or page when printed so: Kendells' POISSONS 15%); a rule on a heading's middle row runs either side of it (Osteria's double red rules, Rucola's dotted DINNER, a knock-out counting for both sides); a rule over the first blocks of a band's columns is the band's top, one crossing a band between its sections splits it into two rows (Rucola's pastas and sides); otherwise it is the top of the first block below in each column, or the bottom of the block above when nothing follows (Sarge's footer note). A vertical rule in a band's gutter covering half its height divides the columns from 60rem up; inside a list it is the list's column rule. A rule crossing a block (table grids, item boxes) is that block's own and is not drawn. Decorative: borders only, kept in forced colours and print. CSS dotted spaces its dots at their width (2 px pitch against Rucola's 2.8 pt): accepted. Only with a measured brand.
- Stand-ins (2026-10-16, `html/webfonts.ts`, library of 35 Google families under packages/pipeline/fonts (Yellowtail, the printed view's brush stand-in, added 2026-10-18; Bebas Neue, Allura, Solway, Averia Libre and Courier Prime the same day: printed on the set and free, so they are themselves; Averia Libre is marked a text face by hand, Google's "display" would keep it off Claudio's running text)): a commercial family takes the free face of its genre: DIN → Barlow (Condensed → Barlow Condensed), Gotham/Baufra/Novecento/Articulat/Semplicita/Proxima/Brandon → Montserrat, Futura/Neutra/Century Gothic → Jost, Verlag → Montserrat, Windsor → Fraunces (2026-10-18), Garamond/Adobe Garamond/Garamond Classic → EB Garamond, Minion → Crimson Pro, Bodoni (BT, MT, FLF, Egyptian) → Bodoni Moda, Didot → Playfair Display, Avenir → Nunito Sans, Myriad → PT Sans, Helvetica/Arial → Arimo, Cambria → Caladea, Calibri → Carlito, Beton → Roboto Slab, Gill Sans → Lato, Inkwell Sans → Lato (2026-10-18: a regular humanist hand; Kalam set Moosewood in a heavy marker hand), Bimbo → Caveat, formal scripts (Snell Roundhand, Edwardian, Zapfino) → Great Vibes; Arizonia, Noto Sans, IBM Plex Sans, Roboto Condensed are themselves. Times has no vendored stand-in (Tinos's licence text is not in Google's repository) and keeps its class stack. The key's rest after the family must be a weight or style word, as for a vendored name. A display face (script, hand-print, stencil) dresses large short headings and dish names set in it, never prices or running text, and on a phone never shrinks below the 1.5rem it was admitted at.
- Small caps (2026-10-16, `html/profile.ts` smallCaps): a role is a small-caps face when at least a quarter of its items (eight or more) show what a small-caps font leaves in the text layer: SC glyphs in the wrong case ("MEatball app") or an initial capital in a larger size touching the rest of the word in smaller capitals ("C" 12pt + "URRIED" 9pt). The role gets `font-variant-caps: small-caps; font-synthesis: small-caps` (the page sets `font-synthesis: none`; the embedded files carry no small-cap glyphs) and its text takes the model's case, so "plantain Chips" renders PLANTAIN CHIPS with a large P and C, as printed; innerText is unchanged. The price role's measured weight reaches the price itself (Almond and Noah's print light prices).
- Sizes: each role keeps its printed size relative to the body text within limits (names and prices 1–1.5×, running text 1–1.25×, details never above the name); headings at least 1.1× the names, at most 2.4rem, shrinking on phones with clamp(); running text in a small-eyed embedded face gets font-size-adjust (ex-height 0.46). A colour that reads only after a large lightness move (below 3:1 before) gives way to the document's other brand colour (its band). Print is black text on white whatever the brand.
- Rules under headings and hairlines between dishes are read from the page render: a thin line through a heading's middle within 1.5 heading heights below it is its rule (drawn as an empty generated box, width as a share of the page, centred when the heading is centred over it); dishes get dividers only when the PDF draws them. An ornament printed right under a heading (a flourish, a rule with arrow ends: one shape of line art that touches and encloses no words) is drawn from the render instead of a plain rule: an alpha mask of the drawing painted in its printed colour (one colour: a two-colour ornament takes its main one), sized in ems of the heading so it keeps its proportion on a phone, the heading centred over it when the PDF centres it; print and forced colours show a plain rule. Ornaments elsewhere, boxed lines, icons and panels are not drawn yet; the brief lists them and `pnpm adhoc` prints those the page lacks.

## Band and links
- One h1: the brand on its own line (18 px, 600) and the document name (24–32 px), from the brief's "Brand · Name"; a screen reader hears a comma between them. 24 px vertical padding. A contents entry that repeats the title is left out.
- Printed web addresses, e-mails and phone numbers become links (`https://`, `mailto:`, `tel:` with the printed digits only; no country code is added). Inside a sentence they are exempt from the target-size minimum; they get vertical padding and space after them.

## Checks per document
Automated, every conversion: hidden text left out and listed in the report; crops clear of neighbouring text; axe at 1280, 390 and 320 px and in forced colours; WCAG 1.4.12 text spacing at 320 px; a keyboard pass (every link and scroll region reached, focus ring visible, nothing under a sticky bar); page markers with the PDF's numbers. Manual, per template and on samples: a screen-reader pass (VoiceOver), conditions read with their prices, links working.

## What reads as cheap or generated (avoid)
Centred everything, a card per dish, dot leaders across a wide screen, emoji or clip-art icons, gradient pills, heavy shadows and large radii, a colour per badge, filler copy, invented percentages, mixed decimals, unlabelled multiple prices, light grey text, ALL-CAPS paragraphs, accordions closed by default (they break Ctrl+F and print), mismatched photos.

## Print
Navigation hidden, one column on white (menus too), a real `thead` repeated on every page, tables not clipped, `break-inside: avoid` on rows, cards and pictures.

## Review
Every page that may reach an owner is reviewed twice: by `pnpm review:page` (the AI reviewer, `model/review.ts`: the PDF's first pages beside our page at 1280 and 390 px; differences by category with a severity; a major one blocks the send list) and by Andrei. The reviewer judges only what the eye sees; words and prices are checked by the validator and must stay as printed.
